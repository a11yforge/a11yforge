How to fix this correctly:
1. The background color stays fixed. Adjust ONLY the foreground (text) color, unless the snippet makes clear the text color must stay and the background is what should change.
2. Move the foreground color in the direction that increases contrast against the background: darker on a light background, lighter on a dark background.
3. Change the color as LITTLE as possible while reaching the requirement. Stay as close to the original color (its hue) as you can — the goal is to preserve the visual design, not to replace the color with black or white. Defaulting to #000000 or #ffffff is a poor fix and must be avoided unless the original color is already almost black or white.
4. The resulting contrast ratio must be AT OR ABOVE the required ratio ({{expectedContrastRatio}}), with a small safety margin. Aim to clear the threshold by a little (e.g. land around 0.1–0.3 above it), so rounding does not push it back under.
5. Preserve all other attributes, classes, and structure. Change only the color value.

Do NOT:
- Default to pure black (#000000) or pure white (#ffffff) out of convenience.
- Increase the font-size or font-weight to qualify for the lower large-text threshold instead of fixing the color.
- Change the text content, the element type, or anything unrelated to the color.
- Produce a color that still fails — the new ratio must meet or exceed {{expectedContrastRatio}}.
